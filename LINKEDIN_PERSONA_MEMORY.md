## What Is Most Likely To Go Viral

The strongest viral formula for Niketh is:

1. Start with a relatable frustration or contrarian observation.
2. Make it specific to LinkedIn, education, AI, or building.
3. Add a screenshot/image or concrete proof.
4. Tie it to a personal stake.
5. End with a simple comment prompt.

High-probability formats:

### Format A: LinkedIn Meta Observation

Hook examples:

- "Is it just me or is LinkedIn starting to feel like every other app?"
- "LinkedIn growth feels different now."
- "I think LinkedIn accidentally rewards the wrong thing."
- "The weirdest thing about posting every day is..."

Why this works: the audience can instantly relate and comment.

### Format B: Education Access Rant

Hook examples:

- "As a UK student, I hate how hidden most opportunities are."
- "Top school admissions is not just about merit."
- "The students who need these programs most are usually the last to hear about them."

Why this works: it has moral tension and invites strong opinions.

### Format C: Builder Result With A Number

Hook examples:

- "We built this in 8 hours."
- "500 students used this and the result surprised me."
- "I tried to fix one Gradlify bug and learned something bigger."

Why this works: numbers create credibility and curiosity.

### Format D: Personal Chaos Into Lesson

Hook examples:

- "I got a nosebleed during training and still kept going."
- "I finally learned to handstand after 16 years."
- "I thought this Gradlify bug was going to ruin everything."

Why this works: vivid personal moments make the post feel real.

### Format E: Tool / AI Workflow

Hook examples:

- "Here's how I stopped hitting my Claude usage limits."
- "I built faster when I stopped writing every line myself."
- "This is the AI workflow I wish I had earlier."

Why this works: practical, timely, useful.

- Pure product updates without tension.
- Abstract motivational posts with no new story.
- Posts that are only "I am happy to announce..."
- Posts that lack a clear opinion.
- Posts where the hook is vague and the payoff is generic.
- Posts that are too polished and lose the personal edge.

Gradlify updates should usually be reframed through:

- A problem parents/students have.
- A failure or obstacle.
- A surprising user behavior.
- A broader unfairness in education.
- A clear number.

Stronger: "A parent messaged me because their child had no idea what to revise next. So I built this."
