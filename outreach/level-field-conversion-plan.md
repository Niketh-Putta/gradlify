# The Level Field Conversion Plan

## Diagnosis

The WhatsApp group is a warm audience, but it has been trained to expect free 11+ material. That means a normal "try Gradlify free" post is likely to create more free users, not payers.

The fix is not more free content. The fix is to use the existing 30-day points sprint as the behaviour path:

1. Parent replies with child year.
2. Parent gets a direct Gradlify link.
3. Child completes one mock exam.
4. Parent sends score/weak area.
5. You give a useful next step for gaining more points.
6. Then you offer Premium as the way to unlock more attempts, larger mocks, and a better chance of staying competitive.

This changes the relationship from "Niketh sends free worksheets" to "Niketh helps me understand what my child should do next."

## WhatsApp Signal Found

The group is named "The Level Field: FREE Daily 11+ material".

The group description says the 11+ system is broken because it rewards families who can pay thousands for private tutors. It links to `https://gradlify.com/free-resources`.

WhatsApp Web only exposes recent web history and says older messages before 23/02/2026 need to be viewed on the phone. Visible recent activity today was mainly member changes, not enough message history to measure engagement by post type.

## Core Campaign

Name: 30-Day Gradlify 11+ Points Sprint

Audience: Year 4 and Year 5 parents in The Level Field.

Promise: "Your child can compete for 30 days, earn points through mock exams, and the highest score wins a £100 Amazon gift card."

Free entry: join the sprint and complete the first mock exam.

Paid conversion: Premium for families whose child is actively competing and wants unlimited mocks, larger sessions, progress tracking, and more attempts to climb the leaderboard.

Primary CTA: "Reply with your child's year: Y4 / Y5 / Y6."

Paid CTA: "Reply PREMIUM."

Tracking link: `https://gradlify.com/11-plus?ref=LEVELFIELD`

Upgrade link: `https://gradlify.com/settings?ref=LEVELFIELD`

## Why This Should Work Better

Parents do not pay because they receive more content. They pay when:

- they feel behind,
- they see a specific weakness,
- they believe consistency is the bottleneck,
- they trust the person giving the recommendation,
- the next step is concrete and low-friction.

The current free-resource model solves "I need materials" but does not create urgency. The 30-day sprint adds urgency, competition, status, and a reason to come back daily.

## 100 User Path From This Group

Assume 600+ members.

Required funnel:

- 600 members see group posts.
- 90 reply with Y4/Y5/Y6 over 7 days.
- 70 click/register.
- 45 complete one mock exam.
- 25 ask for guidance, send scores, or mention leaderboard position.
- 10-20 convert to Premium directly because they want more attempts during the sprint.
- 30-40 more remain active free users who can convert later.

This group alone may not create 100 paid customers immediately, but it can realistically create 50-100 users and the first 10-20 serious paid prospects if handled manually.

## 7-Day Execution

Day 1:

- Send Post 1 from `community-posts.md`.
- DM every parent who replies.
- Log child year, exam timing, and link sent.

Day 2:

- Follow up anyone who replied but did not send a score.
- Send Post 2.

Day 3:

- Summarise weak areas from parent replies.
- Send Post 3 only after real replies exist.

Day 4:

- DM every "PREMIUM" reply.
- Send upgrade link only after explaining Premium.

Day 5:

- Follow up undecided parents with the "too expensive" script if needed.
- Ask active free parents if their child has completed 3 sessions.

Day 6:

- Send Post 4 if there is enough interest.

Day 7:

- Send Post 5 to keep trust.
- Count users, active users, and paid conversions.

## Manual Tracker Columns

Use a sheet or CSV with:

- parent name/number
- child year
- exam area/school
- link sent date
- signed up yes/no
- first mock completed yes/no
- score/weak area
- leaderboard position
- PREMIUM asked yes/no
- paid yes/no
- follow-up date

## Reply Priority

Highest priority:

- "Y5", "exam this year", "struggling", "score", "mock", "leaderboard", "points", "PREMIUM", "price", "how much".

Medium priority:

- "Y4", "later", "send link", "interested".

Low priority:

- generic thanks, emoji reactions, parents who only ask for PDFs.

## What Not To Do

- Do not announce "Premium is available" cold to the whole group.
- Do not keep giving unlimited free PDFs without asking for child year or score.
- Do not sell tutoring replacement. Sell structure, consistency, and visibility.
- Do not make the first CTA "sign up free"; make it "reply with your child's year."
- Do not make the £100 prize the only value. The real conversion reason is more attempts, progress tracking, and knowing what to practise next.
